    What it refuses, on purpose

    A free messenger is free because your patterns pay for it. A private line is paid so nothing has to. The conveniences other apps add all cost the same thing — a server learning something — so a private line does without them.

    • No link previews. To draw the little card, a server has to fetch the link you were sent. Here a link opens nothing until you choose to.
    • No read receipts. Nothing ever announces the moment you opened a message.
    • No typing dots, no online, no last-seen. None of the live feed of when you are there.
    • No cloud backup. Your history is not sitting on anyone's server; nothing is kept once it is delivered.
    • No number, no contacts. There is no account and no address book, so there is no social graph to be the product.

    Every one of those is a convenience given up so the promise stays exactly true: sealed on your phone, held as noise we cannot read, burned on delivery. That is what the price buys — and why those features live on our open surfaces, never here.
